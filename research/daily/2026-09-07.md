# Kaggriculture Daily Intelligence — 2026-09-07

Scope: systematic high-signal sweep of the public Kaggriculture Code and Discussion areas available on 2026-09-07. This is an evidence map, not a claim that every public page on Kaggle has been exhaustively downloaded. Production changes remain blocked until Codex reproduces the result on the current engine.

## Executive conclusion

The public meta is no longer well described as either a fixed 720-turn tape or a fully reactive agent. The strongest public evidence supports a **strong open-loop economic backbone plus a thin conditional routing/repair layer**, while at least one recently observed top agent was genuinely adaptive from very early turns.

For our project this means:

1. Do **not** hand-design a new economy from the current melon baseline.
2. Do **not** assume turns 0–180 are globally solved as a fixed tape.
3. Measure action entropy and lineage on public replays first.
4. Freeze only low-entropy regions; introduce state branches only where conditional entropy shows they matter.
5. Keep a runtime-adaptive challenger in parallel with the schedule champion.

## Discussion meta findings

### 1. Public strategy families form a ladder more than rock-paper-scissors

A public round robin of 14 implementations over 96 fresh seeds with both seats reported:

- no intransitive triple among the tested implementations;
- 86 / 91 chronological pairs were won by the newer implementation;
- adjacent generations typically won 60–80%;
- two generations apart often won 90–100%;
- ordering tracked average final money closely.

The same analysis fingerprints strategy families by the first 120 actions and observes waves of copied openings spreading through the field.

Source:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/739273

Interpretation: for the public meta, **underlying economic quality dominates matchup-specific game theory**. Opponent adaptation still matters at the margin, especially through the market.

### 2. The current top is not necessarily the current strongest agent

Top leaderboard submissions can be obsolete by the time their rating converges, because teams can field newer submissions while old ratings remain visible. Strong agents may also be intentionally retired after validation.

Sources:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/737955
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/739179

Decision: do not treat the displayed top-10 list as a clean ordered target set. Prefer newest public episodes, submission lineage, and head-to-head replay evidence.

### 3. Tape replay is strong, but the best evidence does not support a universal fixed tape

One x-ray study observed a top agent whose route forked around turns 72–144 with the shop draw, while within each shop world only about 0.6–3.9% of turns varied. Another recently observed #1 was genuinely live: divergence before the first shop draw and large measured margins over its recent games.

Sources:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/739179
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/738563

Interpretation: the right abstraction is likely **piecewise-open-loop with sparse conditional branches**, not a globally fixed 180- or 720-turn sequence.

### 4. Strong open-loop backbone + selective adaptation is a recurring independent conclusion

A discussion on the existence of an opponent-agnostic optimum argues that the early game is nearly separable because of market depth, but late shared-market depletion, order timing, and liquidation make payoff opponent-dependent. The proposed architecture is a static production backbone plus selective hysteresis-based adaptation.

Source:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/733173

This aligns with the later replay meta and public notebooks.

### 5. Shared market is the main strategic interaction surface

Participants explicitly point out that the opponent affects your sell/buy prices and order resolution through the shared market even if farm production is mostly self-contained.

Source:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/732613

Decision: opponent-aware work should focus first on market pressure, liquidation, and near-mirror matchups rather than rewriting the entire production plan.

### 6. End-to-end RL is not currently the dominant public solution

Multiple high-ranked participants report heuristics / schedule methods. Public discussion reports poor end-to-end PPO results, moderate hybrid Heuristic+RL results, and some RL work focused on opponent modelling.

Sources:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/736369
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/736917
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/737937

Decision: do not make end-to-end RL the first investment. Use learning/search to discover schedules, routes, or opponent-response gates before trying a fully learned policy.

### 7. Replay data should be treated as a continuously updating dataset

A public notebook/dataset pair archives top-10 replays and can be scheduled daily.

Source:
- https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/737764

Decision: the research pipeline should ingest fresh replay data daily and never assume a strategy family remains current.

## 0–180 hypothesis after the full sweep

The claim “the first 180 steps are solved” remains too strong.

Our working hypothesis is:

- turns 0–~50: very low entropy opening backbone is plausible;
- ~50–72: still likely low entropy, but opponent / execution repair may matter;
- ~72–144: first shop-conditioned route fork is strongly supported by public x-ray evidence;
- ~120–180: additional shop / capital / pasture / market branches likely matter;
- after ~180: route entropy should rise further.

This must be measured, not assumed.

## Quantitative work ordered for Codex

### Q1 — opening entropy map

For every public replay seat:

- canonicalize each action;
- compute `H(A_t)` for t=0..180;
- compute modal action frequency;
- fingerprint first 120 and 180 actions;
- detect lineage / clone families.

Then compute conditional entropy where state is available:

- `H(A_t | shop_signature)`
- `H(A_t | strategy_family)`
- `H(A_t | seat)`

The purpose is to identify exactly which turns can be frozen.

### Q2 — route-fork causal test

For each divergence window, compare final result / money under:

- same backbone, default route;
- shop-conditioned route;
- market-conditioned route;
- opponent-family guard.

Use fresh seeds and both seats.

### Q3 — economic macro curves

For each strategy family estimate by day:

- cash;
- land quadrants;
- hands;
- cow/sheep/goose counts;
- crop mix;
- shed inventory;
- terminal stranded inventory.

Use top vs median vs losing families.

### Q4 — market interaction

Measure near-mirror matches separately. Quantify:

- same-product sell collisions;
- first-seller advantage;
- late liquidation loss;
- hysteresis thresholds;
- opponent-sensitive delta.

### Q5 — runtime adaptive challenger

Maintain a separate challenger that changes only high-entropy decisions while following a proven schedule elsewhere.

Do not promote a full reactive rewrite until it wins this ablation.

## Current project decision

The existing `main.py` melon/wheat baseline is only a pipeline baseline and is not competitive with the public meta. It should not receive extensive parameter tuning.

Next production promotion should come from measured replay/schedule evidence, with this hierarchy:

1. reproduce a strong public economic backbone legally and exactly;
2. validate on fresh seeds / both seats;
3. add shop-conditioned routing;
4. add livestock/pasture timing changes one at a time;
5. add market/terminal hysteresis;
6. compare against a runtime adaptive challenger.

No change to production `main.py` is approved by this research note alone.
